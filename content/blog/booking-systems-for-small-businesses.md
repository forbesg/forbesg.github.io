If customers book appointments with you, online booking can save hours of phone calls
and messages, and lets people book when it suits them, often late in the evening. The
question is whether to use an off-the-shelf booking app or have one built for your
business.

For most small businesses, the honest answer is to start off the shelf. But there's a
point where a custom system pays for itself.

## Off-the-shelf booking apps

There are plenty of ready-made booking systems for salons, clinics, trainers,
tradespeople and more. You sign up, add your services and opening times, and drop a
booking button or widget onto your website.

**Where they shine:**

- **Quick and cheap to start**, often with a free plan or a modest monthly fee.
- **Proven features** like reminders, calendars and card payments.
- **Nothing to build or maintain.**

**What to watch out for:**

- **They work the way they work.** If your business doesn't fit their idea of a
  booking, you end up working around the system.
- **They can feel bolted on**, with a widget that doesn't match the rest of your site.
- **Fees grow with you**, through per-staff, per-booking or commission charges.
- **Your customer data lives in their system**, on their terms.

## When a custom booking system makes sense

A custom system is worth considering when:

- **Your business doesn't fit the standard model**, for example when you go to the
  customer, or bookings involve several people, locations or stages.
- **You have more than one kind of user**, like customers, staff or freelancers and an
  office team, who each need their own view.
- **Payments are unusual**, such as paying a self-employed professional directly or
  taking a deposit now and the balance later.
- **You've outgrown the fees** of an off-the-shelf app.
- **Booking is the heart of your business**, rather than an add-on.

## A real example: Little City Treat

[Little City Treat](/portfolio/web-application/little-city-treat/) brings hair and
beauty treatments to customers' homes, offices and hotels in Edinburgh and Newcastle,
delivered by self-employed beauty professionals. The third-party booking systems
available were all a little too rigid for how the business works, so it started in 2020
with a bespoke booking system.

That's grown into a set of connected apps, one for each of the people who use it:

- **A booking app for customers**, where they choose treatments and book by date or by
  professional, seeing live availability as they go.
- **An app for the professionals**, where they set their own availability and manage
  their calendar and earnings.
- **An admin site** for the team to run the whole business in one place.
- **An advance booking site**, launching soon, for group bookings made well ahead, with
  invoices created through the business's accounting software.

Payments fit the way the business works: the customer's payment is authorised when they
book, but only taken on the night of the appointment, and it goes directly to the
professional, with a fee for the booking.

Because everything runs on serverless functions rather than a server to maintain, the
whole stack costs less than £1.50 a month to run, payment processing fees aside. That's
the other side of custom: once it's built, it doesn't have to be expensive to keep
running.

## Questions to ask yourself

- **What does a booking look like** in your business? Write down every step, from
  enquiry to payment to follow-up.
- **Who needs to see what?** Customers, staff, freelancers, the office.
- **How do you take payment?** Upfront, deposit, on the day, or invoiced.
- **What does your current system cost**, including the hours spent working around it?
- **What other software** does booking need to talk to, like accounts or a calendar?

If an off-the-shelf app handles all of that, use it. If you find yourself listing the
workarounds, a custom system may be worth a conversation.
