The thing that most affects how quickly a new website goes live isn't the design or the
code. It's the content: the words, the photos and the details only you can provide.
With everything ready, a brochure website usually takes me 2 to 3 weeks. Without it,
the build waits.

Here's what to gather before we start.

## Your pages and what goes on them

Make a list of the pages you need. For most small businesses, that's something like:

- **Home:** what you do, who for, and where.
- **One page per service**, so each has its own chance to show up on Google.
- **About:** who you are, and why customers should choose you.
- **Examples of your work**, such as a gallery or case studies.
- **Contact:** phone, email, address or the areas you cover, and opening hours.

## Your words

You know your business better than anyone, so a first draft from you is the best
starting point, even if it's rough. I'll help shape it for the web and for Google.

- **Write how you'd talk to a customer.** Plain words beat jargon.
- **Answer the questions people always ask you.** They make great content and good
  FAQs.
- **Say where you work.** The towns and areas you cover help local customers find you.

If you already have a website, tell me which pages and wording you want to keep.

## Your photos and logo

- **Your logo**, ideally as the original file from your designer: SVG, EPS or AI if you
  have it, or a large PNG.
- **Real photos of your work, your team and your premises.** They build far more trust
  than stock photos.
- **The original files**, not copies from social media, which are often shrunk and
  compressed.

Don't worry about the size of the files. Send them at full size and I'll optimise them
so they load quickly.

## Your domain name

If you already own a domain, find out where it's registered and check you can log in.
I'll need access to its DNS settings to point it at your new site, or you can add the
records yourself. You keep ownership of the domain either way.

If you don't have one yet, I'll register it for you as part of the build, and it's free
for the first year.

## Logins for anything that's moving

If you're replacing an existing site, gather the logins for:

- **Your current website** and hosting.
- **Google Search Console** and **Google Business Profile**, if you have them.
- **Email marketing, booking or shop platforms** that the site needs to connect to.

Moving a site also means redirecting your old page addresses to the new ones, so you
keep your Google rankings. I'll take care of that, but a list of your most important
pages helps.

## Websites you like

Send me links to a few websites you like, and say what you like about them: the colours,
the layout, how easy they are to use. It doesn't matter whether they're in your industry.
It's the quickest way for me to get a feel for what you want.

Links to competitors you'd like to stand out from are useful too.

## Legal pages

Most business websites need a privacy policy, especially if you have a contact form or
collect any customer details. If you take bookings or sell online, you'll also need
terms and conditions. These are your policies, so it's worth getting them from a source
you trust.

## You don't need everything on day one

If something isn't ready, we can start with what you have and add the rest as it comes.
But the more that's ready at the start, the sooner your site goes live.
