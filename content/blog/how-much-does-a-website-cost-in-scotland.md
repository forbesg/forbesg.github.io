## What changes the price

**How many pages and layouts you need.** A five-page site for a tradesperson is a very
different job from a fifteen-page site with a gallery, team pages and a blog. Layouts
matter more than page count: ten service pages that share one design cost less than
ten pages that each look different.

**Whether you want to edit it yourself.** If you're happy to send me the odd change, a
simple static site keeps costs down. If you'll be adding news, offers or menus every
week, a site with a content editor (a CMS) is worth the extra, and saves you paying for
changes later.

**Selling online.** An online shop needs products, a cart, a checkout, payments and
order emails, so it's a bigger build. The scope is what sets the price: how many
products you have, how you take payment, and whether the shop connects to anything
else, like stock or delivery systems, or trade accounts with their own pricing.

**Having your content ready.** Your text and photos are the biggest factor in how
quickly a site comes together. If they're ready, a brochure site usually takes 2 to 3
weeks.

**Anything bespoke.** Booking systems, customer accounts and connections to other
software are quoted on their own, because no two are the same.

## Running costs to budget for

The build is the big cost, but a website has a few running costs too. Here's what to
expect with me, so there are no surprises:

- **Domain name and hosting.** Free for the first year. After that there's a yearly
  charge, which I'll set out in your quote. If you already own your domain, you keep it
  and there's no domain charge at all.
- **A content editor (CMS).** Many headless CMSs have free or very low cost plans for
  small businesses. You'll know about any subscription before we start.
- **Online shops.** Your ecommerce platform's monthly plan, and the card fees charged
  by payment providers like Stripe or Shopify Payments, are paid directly to them.
- **Changes after launch.** Small changes are free for the first 30 days. After that,
  any changes are quoted up front.

## Website builder, freelancer or agency?

**Doing it yourself with a website builder** costs the least up front, but you pay a
monthly fee for as long as the site exists, and the time to build and look after it is
yours. Builder sites can also be slower and harder to optimise for Google, and if
yours isn't performing, that's what my [website rescue](/services/website-rescue/)
service is for.

**An agency** brings a bigger team, which suits large projects, but you're also paying
for account managers, project managers and the office. Your website is often handed
between several people along the way.

**A freelancer** like me sits in between. You deal with the person who builds your
site from the first chat to launch day and every change after, and you don't pay for
layers of management.

## How paying works

Brochure and CMS websites are paid half to get started and the rest when the site goes
live. Online shops are paid in stages: half to start, a quarter when you've signed off
the preview, and the rest at launch.
