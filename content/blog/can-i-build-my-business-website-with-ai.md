It's a fair question, and one I get asked more and more: if AI can build a website in a
few minutes, why pay someone to do it?

I'm a freelance web developer, so you'd expect me to tell you not to bother with AI. But
I use AI tools every day to build sites, including this one. They've made me faster, and
they're good. So here's the honest answer: for some businesses, AI is the right choice.
For others, it'll cost more in the long run than hiring someone would have.

## Two very different kinds of "AI website"

When people say they'll build a website with AI, they usually mean one of two things.

**AI website builders.** These are the website builder platforms you've heard of, now
with AI built in. You describe your business, and they produce a site with pages, text
and images in a few minutes. You then edit it in their drag-and-drop editor and pay a
monthly subscription to keep it online.

**AI coding tools.** These write the actual code of a website, or a whole app, from a
conversation. They're far more flexible, but what you get is code. Someone has to host
it, look after it and fix it when something goes wrong, and that someone is usually you.

They have different strengths and different risks, so it's worth knowing which one you
mean.

## Where AI does a good job

- **It's fast.** You can have something online the same day.
- **It's cheap to start.** A builder subscription is usually a small monthly cost,
  compared with paying a designer up front.
- **It gets you past the blank page.** AI is good at a first draft of your pages and
  text, even if you rewrite most of it.
- **It's fine for simple needs.** One page with what you do, where you are and how to
  get in touch is well within what AI can manage.

## Where AI falls short

### Looking like everyone else

AI builders work from templates, and AI-written text tends to sound the same. Phrases
like "passionate about delivering exceptional service" don't tell a customer anything.
If your competitors are using the same tools, your site can end up looking and sounding
just like theirs.

### Getting found on Google

A site that exists isn't the same as a site that gets found. AI will give you pages, but
it won't plan which local searches each page should target, set up Google Search Console
or your Google Business Profile, or check that Google is indexing what it should. I've
written more about this in
[how to get your business onto Google in Edinburgh](/blog/how-to-get-your-business-on-google-in-edinburgh/).

### Speed

Builder sites are often heavy, and AI tools tend to add more than they need. Most local
searches happen on a phone, so a slow site loses customers before they've seen it. See
[why is my website slow?](/blog/why-is-my-website-slow/) for what to look out for.

### UK rules

AI tools don't know your business, and most of them aren't written with the UK in mind.
A privacy policy, cookie banner or terms of sale written by AI can look convincing and
still be wrong for UK GDPR. Check them carefully, or have someone who knows check them
for you.

### When something breaks

This is the big one with AI coding tools. AI is great at writing code and much less good
at fixing it. If you don't understand the code, you can't tell whether the fix it
suggests is right, and each "fix" can break something else. I've seen people get a site
90% of the way there in an afternoon, then spend weeks on the last 10%.

### Payments, bookings and anything that holds customer data

Taking payments, managing bookings or storing customer details is where mistakes cost
real money. That's where it pays to have someone who has done it before, whether that's
a well-established platform or a developer. My posts on
[Shopify or a custom online shop](/blog/shopify-or-a-custom-online-shop/) and
[booking systems for small businesses](/blog/booking-systems-for-small-businesses/) go
into the options.

### You may not own it

With an AI builder, your site lives on their platform. If you stop paying, it goes
offline, and moving it elsewhere usually means starting again. That's fine if you're
happy to stay, but it's worth knowing before you put in the work.

## When AI is the right choice

Go ahead and use AI if:

- **You're just starting out** and need something online now, on a small budget.
- **You need a simple one-page site** with your services, location and contact details.
- **You're testing an idea** before deciding whether to invest in it.
- **You enjoy doing it yourself** and have the time to learn and look after it.

That's a perfectly good place to start. Plenty of businesses outgrow it later, and
that's fine too.

## When it's worth hiring someone

It's usually worth paying for help if:

- **Your website brings in your customers**, so how it looks and how well it ranks
  directly affects your income.
- **You need to stand out** from competitors in a crowded local market.
- **You need online payments, bookings or customer logins.**
- **You'd rather spend your time running your business** than learning and maintaining
  a website.

My post on [how much a website costs in Scotland](/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost-in-scotland/)
sets out what to budget for, so you can compare it with doing it yourself.

## The middle ground: start with AI, get it checked

It doesn't have to be one or the other. A good option for many small businesses is to
build a first version with AI, then pay a professional for a few hours to review and
finish it: tidy up the text, fix the speed, set it up for local search and make sure it
works properly on phones.

just as well for a site you've built yourself with AI as for an old site that's got slow.

customers, deciding what each page should do, and checking that the finished site is
