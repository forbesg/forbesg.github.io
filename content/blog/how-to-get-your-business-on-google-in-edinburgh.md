When someone in Edinburgh searches for "plumber near me" or "cafe in Leith", Google
shows a map with a handful of local businesses, then the ordinary search results
underneath. Getting into those results isn't a dark art. Most of it comes down to a few
things done properly and kept up to date, and you can do much of it yourself.

Here's the checklist I work through with clients.

## 1. Set up your Google Business Profile

Your [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) is the listing that
appears in the map results and on the right-hand side when someone searches for your
name. It's free, and for a local business it's the single most important thing on this
list.

- **Claim and verify it.** Search for your business on Google. If a listing already
  exists, claim it; if not, create one. Google will ask you to verify that you run the
  business before the profile goes live.
- **Choose the right primary category.** This has a big say in which searches you show
  up for. Pick the one that best describes what you do, then add secondary categories
  for anything else.
- **Fill in everything.** Opening hours, phone number, website, services, a short
  description and photos of your work, your premises or your team. A complete profile
  gives Google, and customers, more reasons to choose you.
- **Set your service area.** If you visit customers rather than having them come to
  you, you can hide your address and list the areas you cover instead, such as
  Edinburgh, Leith, Musselburgh or the Lothians.

Google says local results are mainly based on three things: how relevant your listing
is to the search, how far you are from the person searching, and how well known your
business is. You can't change where you are, but the other two are up to you.

## 2. Keep your name, address and phone number consistent

Google cross-checks your details across the web. Make sure your business name, address
and phone number are written the same way on your website, your Business Profile,
Facebook, and directories like Yell, Thomson Local or trade sites like Checkatrade.

If you've moved premises or changed number, track down the old listings and update
them. Mismatched details make it harder for Google to be sure which business is which.

## 3. Ask for reviews, and reply to them

Reviews help you stand out in the map results, and they're often what makes someone
pick you over the business next door.

- **Ask every happy customer.** The best moment is just after a job goes well. Your
  Business Profile gives you a short link you can text or email to customers.
- **Reply to every review**, good or bad. A polite, helpful reply to a poor review
  often reassures people more than a page of five stars.
- **Don't buy reviews or offer anything in return for them.** It's against Google's
  rules, and listings can be penalised for it.

## 4. Make your website clear about where you are and what you do

Your Business Profile and your website work together. Google looks at your site to
understand what you offer and where.

- **Say it plainly.** Your page titles and headings should say what you do and where,
  in the words customers use: "Electrician in Edinburgh" works better than
  "Welcome to our website".
- **Give each service its own page.** One page for boiler repairs and another for
  bathroom fitting gives Google a clear page to show for each search, rather than one
  page trying to cover everything.
- **Mention the areas you cover**, naturally, where it's relevant. If you work across
  Edinburgh and the Lothians, say so. Don't stuff in a list of every postcode.
- **Put your contact details on every page**, usually in the footer, matching your
  Business Profile exactly.
- **Add structured data.** This is a small piece of code that tells Google your
  business name, address, phone number and opening hours in a format it can read
  directly. Your visitors never see it, but search engines do.

## 5. Make sure your site is fast and works on a phone

Most local searches happen on a phone, often when someone needs you now. If your site
takes an age to load or is awkward to use on a small screen, people head back to the
results and pick someone else.

You can test any page with Google's free [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/)
tool. It scores your page and lists what's slowing it down. If the scores are low, the
usual culprits are large images, too many plugins and heavy third-party scripts.

## 6. Set up Google Search Console

[Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/) is free, and it shows
you what Google sees: which searches you appear for, how often people click, and
whether any of your pages have problems being indexed.

Once it's set up, submit your sitemap so Google can find all your pages, then check in
every month or so. It's the best way to see whether the changes you make are working.

## 7. Keep it up

Local search rewards businesses that look active. Add new photos to your Business
Profile from time to time, keep your hours right over holidays, reply to reviews, and
add to your website when you start offering something new. A little, regularly, beats
a big push once a year.
