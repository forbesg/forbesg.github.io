If you want to sell online, Shopify is probably the first name you'll come across. It's
a good platform, and for many small businesses it's the right answer. But it isn't the
only one, and sometimes a custom shop will serve you better.

There's also a middle option that gets the best of both. Here's how they compare.

## Shopify on its own

Shopify gives you a complete shop out of the box: products, a basket, checkout,
payments, order emails and a back office to run it all. You pick a theme, add your
products and you can be selling quickly.

**Where it shines:**

- **Getting started fast**, with a standard design.
- **A familiar, well-tested back office**, with plenty of guides and support.
- **A huge library of apps** for reviews, subscriptions, shipping and more.

**What to watch out for:**

- **Monthly costs add up.** You pay a monthly plan, and many apps have their own monthly
  fees on top. Shopify also adds transaction fees if you take payment through a provider
  other than Shopify Payments.
- **Your shop looks and works the way the theme allows.** Changing that means editing
  the theme's code or buying another app.
- **Speed can suffer** as themes and apps load more and more code onto every page.

## A custom shop

A custom shop is designed and built around your business. Under the hood, it usually
uses a headless ecommerce platform for the products, baskets and orders, with a fast
shop front built exactly how you want it. I explain how that works in
[Can an Ecommerce Website Be Static?](/blog/can-an-ecommerce-site-be-static/)

**Where it shines:**

- **Things standard shops find awkward.** Trade accounts, custom pricing, products made
  to measure, or a buying process that doesn't fit a standard checkout.
- **Speed**, because the shop only loads what it needs.
- **A design that's yours**, not a theme thousands of other shops use.

**What to watch out for:**

- **A bigger build up front**, because more of it is made for you.
- **You rely on a developer** for new features, rather than installing an app.

A real example: [GSD Trade](/portfolio/ecommerce/gsd-trade/) is a trade-only shop where
customers log in and each account sees its own tiered pricing. That's the kind of thing
that's hard to bend a standard theme around, but straightforward when the shop is built
for the job.

## The middle option: Shopify behind a custom shop front

You can keep Shopify's back office for products, orders and payments, and put a fast,
custom-built shop front in front of it, using Shopify's Storefront API. Your team gets a
familiar back office, and your customers get a shop that's quick and designed around
your brand.

I wrote about how this works in
[A Static Ecommerce Website with Shopify](/blog/a-static-ecommerce-website-with-shopify/).

## How to choose

**Go with Shopify on its own if** you want to start selling soon, you're happy with a
standard design, and the apps cover everything you need.

**Go custom if** your business has requirements that don't fit a standard shop, like
trade pricing or a different way of ordering, or speed and a distinctive design are
central to how you sell.

**Go for the middle option if** you like Shopify's back office but want a faster shop
front that looks like your brand.

Whichever you choose, your platform's monthly plan and the card fees charged by payment
providers are paid directly to them, so it's worth comparing those too.
